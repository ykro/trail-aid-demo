# Heat illness steps

1. Stop all activity now. Do not push on to the next rest point.
2. Move the person to shade. If there is no shade, make it with a tarp, jacket, or umbrella.
3. Lie the person down and raise the legs slightly.
4. Ask simple questions: name, where they are, what day it is. Watch how they walk and talk.
5. If the person is confused, acting strangely, staggering, having a seizure, or unresponsive, this is heat stroke. Call emergency services now and keep cooling.
6. If the person is alert but sweating heavily, dizzy, cramping, nauseated, or weak, this is heat exhaustion. Cool and rehydrate.
7. Remove extra clothing, pack, hat, and boots.
8. Pour water over the head, neck, and body. Soak clothing. Do not save the water. Use what you have.
9. Fan the person hard with a map, jacket, or your hands. Evaporation is the main cooling method.
10. If there is a stream or lake, sit the person in the cool water up to the neck. Support the head. This is the fastest way to cool a heat stroke.
11. Put wet cloths or cold bottles on the neck, armpits, and groin.
12. If the person is alert and can swallow, give cool water or an electrolyte drink in small sips. Add a pinch of salt or a salty snack.
13. Do not give fluids to anyone who is confused, vomiting, or drowsy.
14. Ask the app to start a timer named "cooling" for thirty minutes and recheck when it ends.
15. Keep cooling until the person feels normal, thinks clearly, and skin feels cool. Then stop cooling so they do not get too cold.
16. If the person becomes unresponsive, check breathing. Start CPR if not breathing.
17. After heat exhaustion, rest in shade for the rest of the day. Do not resume hiking in the heat.
18. After heat stroke, evacuate and get medical care even if the person seems to recover.
