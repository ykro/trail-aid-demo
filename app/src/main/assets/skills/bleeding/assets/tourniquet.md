# Tourniquet

Use a tourniquet only for life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg that direct pressure cannot control. It causes pain and can damage the limb, but it saves lives.

1. Call emergency services now if you have not already.
2. Use a commercial tourniquet if you have one. Otherwise use a strap, belt, or a folded cloth at least five centimeters wide. Never use cord, wire, or a shoelace.
3. Place the tourniquet five to seven centimeters above the wound, on the side closer to the body.
4. Never place it over a joint. If the wound is near the elbow or knee, go above the joint.
5. Place it over bare skin or thin clothing. Remove bulky items from under it.
6. Pull the strap as tight as you can. Then twist the rod or stick until the bleeding stops.
7. For an improvised tourniquet, tie the cloth, slide a stick under the knot, twist until bleeding stops, and tie the stick in place.
8. Secure the rod so it cannot unwind.
9. Ask the app to start a timer named "tourniquet". Note the time. Write it on the person's skin or on the tourniquet if you can.
10. If bleeding continues, tighten more or add a second tourniquet just above the first.
11. Never loosen or remove the tourniquet. Only medical professionals should do that.
12. Cover the wound with a dressing and keep the person warm.
13. Tell rescuers where the tourniquet is and when it was applied.
14. Send your location to your emergency contact and evacuate as fast as possible.
