# Bite and sting steps

Insect stings

1. Move away from the area calmly. Bees and wasps defend their nest.
2. If a stinger is visible, scrape it out sideways with a fingernail or card. Do not squeeze it.
3. Wash the area with soap and water.
4. Apply something cold wrapped in cloth for ten minutes at a time.
5. Take an antihistamine the person normally uses, if alert and not allergic.
6. Ask the app to start a timer named "observe" for thirty minutes. Watch for a reaction.

Allergic reaction

1. Watch for trouble breathing, wheezing, throat tightness, swelling of the lips, tongue, or face, hives spreading over the body, vomiting, dizziness, or fainting.
2. If any of these appear, this is anaphylaxis. Call emergency services now.
3. If the person carries prescribed epinephrine, help them use it right away. Press the auto-injector firmly into the outer thigh and hold for three seconds.
4. Lay the person down with legs raised. If breathing is hard, let them sit up.
5. If symptoms do not improve in five to fifteen minutes and a second dose is available, give it.
6. Give an antihistamine if the person can swallow. It does not replace epinephrine.
7. Stay with the person. Symptoms can return. Evacuate to medical care even if they improve.
8. If the person becomes unresponsive and stops breathing, start CPR.

Tick removal

1. Use fine tweezers. Grasp the tick as close to the skin as possible.
2. Pull straight out with steady, even pressure. Do not twist or jerk.
3. Do not burn the tick, cover it with oil, or squeeze its body.
4. Wash the bite and your hands with soap and water.
5. Note the date and place of the bite. A photo helps.
6. Watch for a spreading rash, fever, or flu-like symptoms over the next weeks and see a clinician if they appear.

Other bites

1. For spider or animal bites, wash well with soap and water and cover with a clean dressing.
2. Any animal bite that breaks the skin needs medical care for infection and rabies risk.
