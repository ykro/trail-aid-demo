# Snake bite

Most snake bites are survivable with calm, stillness, and fast evacuation. Time to a hospital with antivenom is what matters.

1. Move everyone away from the snake. Do not try to catch, kill, or photograph it up close. A description of color and pattern is enough.
2. Call emergency services now. Ask the app to send your location to your emergency contact.
3. Keep the person calm and as still as possible. Fear and movement spread venom faster.
4. Sit or lie the person down with the bitten limb resting at about heart level.
5. Remove rings, watches, bracelets, and tight clothing near the bite before swelling starts.
6. Wash the bite gently with soap and water if available. Cover with a clean, dry dressing.
7. Immobilize the limb with a splint or a sling, the same as for a fracture. Keep it still.
8. Do not cut the wound. Do not try to suck out venom. Do not use a suction device.
9. Do not apply a tourniquet or a tight band.
10. Do not apply ice or cold.
11. Do not give alcohol, caffeine, or pain medication like aspirin or ibuprofen.
12. With a pen, draw a line at the edge of the swelling and write the time next to it.
13. Ask the app to start a timer named "swelling" for fifteen minutes. When it ends, mark the new edge and the time. Repeat. This shows rescuers how fast it is spreading.
14. Watch for trouble breathing, weakness, drooping eyelids, vomiting, or bleeding from the gums. Tell rescuers about any of these.
15. Evacuate as soon as possible. If the person must walk out and no carry is possible, walk slowly with frequent rest.
16. Even if there is no pain or swelling, go to a hospital. Some venom acts hours later.
