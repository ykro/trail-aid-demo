# Improvised splint

A splint keeps the injury still so it hurts less and does not get worse during evacuation. Splint in the position you find the limb.

1. Gather rigid items: trekking poles, straight sticks, a rolled sleeping pad, tent poles, or a folded map.
2. Gather padding: clothing, a fleece, socks, or a towel.
3. Gather ties: bandanas, straps, tape, torn cloth, or pack straps.
4. Check circulation, warmth, and feeling below the injury before you start.
5. Pad the rigid item well so it does not press on bone or skin.
6. Place the padded splint along the limb. It must reach past the joint above the injury and the joint below.
7. For a forearm, immobilize the wrist and the elbow. For a lower leg, immobilize the ankle and the knee.
8. Fill hollow spots, such as behind the knee or under the ankle, with padding so the limb rests naturally.
9. Tie the splint on above and below the injury, never directly over it.
10. Tie snugly but not tight. You should be able to slip one finger under each tie.
11. For an arm, add a sling from a shirt or bandana and bind the arm to the chest.
12. For an ankle, a rolled sleeping pad wrapped in a U shape around the foot and lower leg works well.
13. For a leg, you can also pad between the legs and tie the injured leg to the good leg.
14. Recheck circulation, warmth, and feeling after tying. Loosen ties if fingers or toes go cold, pale, or numb.
15. Recheck every fifteen minutes during evacuation.
16. Keep the limb elevated when resting.
17. Send your location to your emergency contact before you start moving.
