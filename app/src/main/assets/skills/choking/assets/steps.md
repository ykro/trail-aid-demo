# Choking steps

1. Ask "Are you choking? Can you speak?"
2. If the person can speak, cough forcefully, or breathe, the obstruction is mild. Encourage them to keep coughing. Stay with them. Do not slap their back or give thrusts.
3. If the person cannot speak, cannot cough, is making high-pitched sounds, or is turning blue, the obstruction is severe. Act now.
4. If someone else is present, have them call emergency services.
5. Stand behind the person and slightly to one side. Support their chest with one hand and lean them forward.
6. Give five sharp back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
7. Check after each blow whether the object has come out.
8. If it has not, give five abdominal thrusts. Stand behind the person. Wrap your arms around their waist.
9. Make a fist and place it thumb side in, just above the navel and below the ribs.
10. Grab your fist with your other hand. Pull sharply inward and upward, as if lifting the person.
11. Repeat five times, checking after each thrust.
12. If the person is pregnant or very large, give chest thrusts instead. Place your fist on the center of the breastbone and pull straight back.
13. Keep alternating five back blows and five abdominal thrusts until the object comes out or the person becomes unresponsive.
14. If the person becomes unresponsive, lower them gently to the ground on their back.
15. Call emergency services now if not already done. Put the phone on speaker.
16. Start CPR beginning with thirty chest compressions. Ask the app for the CPR steps and metronome.
17. Each time you open the airway to give breaths, look in the mouth. Remove the object only if you can see it. Never sweep blindly with a finger.
18. Continue CPR until the person breathes, help arrives, or you cannot continue.
19. If you are alone and choking, press your own abdomen against a hard edge such as a rock, log, or the back of a chair, thrusting inward and upward.
20. After the object comes out, watch for continued coughing, difficulty swallowing, or belly pain. Anyone who received abdominal thrusts should see a clinician.
